        How do we know?

Here are the dirty little secrets — and the real reasons prices vary.

      

      
    


    

      
Limited market data leads to uneducated consumers

      
    
        
  • 
          No MSRP, joke is on me.
          Cars have sticker prices and invoice data. HVAC has no widely available consumer reference for what your installed system should cost.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          No "uncle" in the biz & your "neighbor" is useless.
          Most homeowners have nobody to call and ask, "Is this actually a fair price?" And the neighbor who replaced theirs bought a different year, system, efficiency, refrigerant and house — their old price tells you surprisingly little about yours.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Frankly, you're no help either.
          But it's not your fault — you buy one every ten years, maybe four in a lifetime. The contractor prices systems every day. You don't.
        
    • 
      

    


    

      
Why two people can pay different prices

      
    
        
  • 
          Nice neighborhood, nice markup.
          ZIP code, neighborhood, home value, urgency, and what a customer appears willing to pay can all influence pricing. Inside the trade, we've heard it called "over the bridge," "the other side of the tracks," or the "rich guy tax" — different ways of saying the same thing: the equipment cost didn't change, but the price to the customer did.
        
    • 
      

    


    

      
Extra costs passed onto you

      
    
        
  • 
          Beware the snake oil salesman.
          There are good AC companies that repair when it makes sense and replace when it's time. Then there are sales-driven companies where "minimum-wage technicians" are really commissioned salespeople with high quotas to close — where commissions can exceed 20% of the sale and drive unethical behavior. When your paycheck depends on selling new equipment, repairing the old one gets thrown out the window — and that commission is ultimately baked into your price. Unfortunately, the industry is rapidly moving toward this sales-first model, making it increasingly difficult for honest, service-first mom-and-pop contractors to compete.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          That click came with a price tag.
          Digital leads can cost contractors hundreds of dollars, and that acquisition cost gets built into pricing. A $500 lead is a lot easier to absorb on a $15,000 replacement than a $500 repair — which can create a powerful incentive to sell replacement over repair.
        
    • 
        
  • 
          Does your AC company have hungry shareholders?
          Some private-equity acquisitions involve significant debt and aggressive growth targets — costs and expectations that can contribute to higher-priced quotes. In some PE-backed models, the push for higher revenue, margins, and an eventual profitable exit may also create stronger incentives for replacement over repair.
        
    • 
      

    


    

      
Good contractors deserve to make an honest living.

      

        We're not against contractors making money — quite the opposite. Good contractors have
        real costs: skilled employees, trucks, insurance, licensing, training, warranties,
        inventory, and overhead. They deserve to earn a fair profit and build a healthy business
        that's still there when you need them five years from now.
      

      

        There's a difference between earning a healthy profit and paying more than you need to.
        Using the Open Book AC Price Decoder helps bring transparency and balance back to the
        industry. Consumers gain the knowledge to recognize a fair price, while good contractors
        can earn a reasonable profit for quality work.
      

      

        Fair for the homeowner. Fair for the contractor. That's the balance we're after.
